1Purpose

This document outlines how we find the center of gravity (cg) height of ARG26 through a physical test. Although we can make reasonable estimates through CAD, validating the cg height is important because it influences a lot of our load transfer and handling characteristics.

There are two main independent methods to calculate the cg height:

  1. Method A: Side Tilt (Balance) Test. The car is tipped onto the tires of one side until it balances. The balance angle gives the CG height directly.
  2. Method B: Front Lift (Axle Load) Test. The front of the car is lifted while the rear tires sit on scales. The change in rear axle load gives the CG height.

2Method A: Side Tilt (Balance) Test

2.1Theory

A body balances on a pivot when its CG is directly above the pivot. In this test the car is tipped sideways onto the tires of one side, so it rests only on the outer edges of those two tires. The car is slowly rotated until it balances and can be held in place with a couple of fingers, with the crane chain slack. At that point the CG lies on the vertical line through the pivot.

In the car's own frame, the CG sits half the car's width, d / 2, in from the pivot edge and a height h above the ground plane. When the car is rotated by the balance angle θ, the line from the pivot to the CG is vertical, so tan θ = (d / 2) / h.

2.3Pre-Test Procedure

  1. Install the accumulator and bolt it down (ask someone on high voltage).
  2. Install the dummy shocks and dummy cones.
  3. Make sure the car is on the magnesium rims.
  4. Overinflate the tires to 30 psi.
  5. Once the car is on the ground, measure the ride height. It will be larger than usual because the dummy shocks are in.
  6. Put the digital level on a flat, visible part of the car and zero it.

2.5Equations

The front and rear of the car have different widths, so the width is taken at the lengthwise position of the CG (Figure 2) [2]. The CG sits a distance a behind the front axle, set by the fraction of weight on the rear axle, WR = WRL + WRR:

a = L × WR / W

d = df + (dr − df) × WR / W

CG height above the ground at static ride height:

h = (d / 2) / tan θ

where θ is the digital level reading at balance (the level is zeroed with the car flat on the ground). This assumes the CG is on the car's centerline.

Before lifting, estimate the angle the car should balance at from the expected CG height (for example, from CAD), so you know roughly where to stop the crane:

θexpected = arctan( (d / 2) / hexpected )

Ride Height Correction

The dummy shocks hold the car above its normal ride height, so the measured h is the CG at the tested ride height. Only the sprung mass moves with ride height, so the CG at the design ride height is:

hnominal = hmeasured − (msprung / mtotal) × (RHtested − RHnominal)

The driver is part of the sprung mass, so the sprung fraction is different for each case. With Wunsprung the same in every case:

W = Wcar + Wdriver,  msprung / mtotal = (W − Wunsprung) / W

Show derivation

Split the car into its sprung mass (chassis, driver, accumulator, powertrain) and unsprung mass (wheels, tires, uprights). The CG height is the mass-weighted average of the parts:

h = Σ(mi ri) / Σ mi

As tested, where rsprung and runsprung are the CG heights of the sprung and unsprung mass:

hmeasured = ( msprung rsprung + munsprung runsprung ) / mtotal

Changing the ride height by ΔRH moves only the sprung mass. The wheels, tires and uprights stay where they are:

hnew = ( msprung (rsprung + ΔRH) + munsprung runsprung ) / mtotal

hnew = hmeasured + (msprung / mtotal) × ΔRH

where ΔRH = RHnew − RHtested. Going from the tested ride height down to the design ride height, ΔRH = −(RHtested − RHnominal), which gives the equation above. The mass ratio msprung / mtotal is the same as the weight ratio Wsprung / W.

An error in θ changes h by roughly Δh ≈ (d / 2) × Δθ / sin2θ (Δθ in radians). A 0.2° error gave roughly a 0.1 in error in h for the car measured in [2], so balance the car several times and average.

2.6Procedure

  1. Prepare the car by following the pre-test procedure.
  2. Wrap the chain around one front rim and one rear rim on the same side, and close it with the maillons.
  3. Attach the crane hook to the chain you just wrapped.
  4. With the crane, raise the car slowly toward the expected angle from the expected CG height, watching the digital level. Use θexpected = arctan( (d / 2) / hexpected ).
  5. Stand by the driver's seat as the car tilts. Put one hand on the roll hoop and one hand on the inner side of the monocoque. These are the points where you apply force to slowly balance the car.
  6. Have someone record a video of the digital level while you teeter the car back and forth. When you get it to balance at the right point, call out a word. This lets us go back through the video and read exactly what angle was measured.
  7. Keep the video going and balance the car several more times to get multiple data points (about 5). Someone else can also teeter the car to get another source of data points.

Use this procedure to measure three cases:

  1. Without the driver
  2. With driver 1
  3. With driver 2

3Method B: Front Lift (Axle Load) Test

In progressMethod B is not finished yet.

3.1Theory

With the rear tires on the scales, the front of the car is lifted by the crane so the car pivots about the rear axle. As the front rises, the CG moves rearward relative to the rear axle, so the rear scales read more weight. The higher the CG sits above the axle line, the larger that increase is. Measuring how much the rear axle load increases for a known change in angle gives the CG height [1].

3.3Pre-Test Procedure

  1. Install the dummy shocks and dummy cones.
  2. Install the accumulator and bolt it down (ask someone on high voltage).
  3. Make sure the car is on the magnesium rims.
  4. Overinflate the tires to 30 psi.
  5. Check each scale with the known weight: less than 0.5 lbf of variance among the scales, and less than 0.5 lbf of error from the known weight, per 50 lb added.
  6. Set the rear scales on the cinder blocks, then level and zero them.
  7. Chock the rear wheels.

3.5Derivation

The car does not need a 50/50 weight split. Let a be the distance from the CG to the front axle and b the distance from the CG to the rear axle, measured along the axle line, so a + b = L (Figure 5). The CG sits a height ha above that line.

With the front raised by an angle α, the horizontal distance from the rear axle to the CG is:

x = b cos α − ha sin α

The crane force F acts at the front axle, a horizontal distance L cos α from the rear axle. Taking moments about the rear axle:

F × L cos α = W × (b cos α − ha sin α)

F = W × b / L − W × (ha / L) × tan α

The rear scales carry the rest of the weight, WR = W − F. Using a + b = L:

WR = W × a / L + W × (ha / L) × tan α

With the car level (α = 0) this is just the static rear weight, WR1 = W × a / L. The weight split only sets that starting reading. Subtracting it leaves the change in rear weight, which depends only on ha:

WRn − WR1 = W × (ha / L) × tan(αn − α1)

3.6Equations

Rear axle weight at each position n:

WRn = WRLn + WRRn

CG height above the axle centerline:

ha = L × (WRn − WR1) / ( W × tan(αn − α1) )

CG height above the ground at static ride height, adding the average loaded radius:

h = ha + (rf + rr) / 2

The level corner weights also locate the CG along the car: a = L × WR1 / W and b = L × WF1 / W.

The change in rear weight is small compared with the total weight, so scale error can become a large part of the result [2]. Use large angles (20° to 40° of change) and average several measurements [3]. The tires also deform under load, lowering the CG slightly, and the value is not exact at speed because of aero loads and tire pressure changes [3].

3.7Procedure

  1. Complete the pre-test procedure and the measurements before lifting.
  2. Lift the front of the car with the crane, keeping the chain vertical.
  3. Hold the car still and record the level reading αn and the rear corner weights WRLn and WRRn.
  4. Repeat at several angles, aiming for about 10°, 20°, 30° and 40° of change.
  5. Lower the car, re-zero the scales, and repeat the full set for a second run.
