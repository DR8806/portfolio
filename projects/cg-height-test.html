CG Height Test

Test procedure for measuring the CG height of ARG26 with two methods: a side tilt test and a rear lift test.

ARG26 · Suspension · Test Procedure · Prepared by Davis Rattanavijai

1Purpose

This protocol details how to determine the height of the center of gravity (CG) of ARG26 above the ground at static ride height. We can make reasonable estimates of the CG height through CAD, but validating those estimates is important to make sure there is good fidelity between our designs and the vehicle itself. If there is a discrepancy between the measured and designed values, weight transfer, roll, pitch and tire force capability will all differ from what the suspension was designed around.

This procedure contains two independent methods so the results can be cross-checked:

  • Method A: Side Tilt (Balance) Test. The car is tipped onto the tires of one side until it balances. The balance angle gives the CG height directly.
  • Method B: Rear Lift (Axle Load) Test. The rear of the car is lifted while the front tires sit on scales. The change in front axle load gives the CG height.

Both methods share the same materials, pre-test setup and baseline measurements (Sections 2 to 4).

3Pre-Testing Procedure

Ensure that all scales are reading properly by placing a known weight on each and recording the displayed value. There must be less than 0.5 lbf of variance among the scales per 50 lb added, and less than 0.5 lbf of error per 50 lb added compared with the known weight.

The car must also be locked at static ride height so that no suspension travel happens during the test. From Analysis Techniques for Racecar Data Acquisition [1]:

  • Each shock absorber should be replaced by a solid link (dummy shock) set to the exact static ride height. Inaccuracy here can greatly influence the results.
  • The tires should be overinflated as much as possible to limit sidewall flexing.
  • All fluids should be at the right level.

Because ARG26 will be tilted to large angles, the accumulator and all loose items must be secured, and the high voltage system must be de-energized before the car is lifted.

5Method A: Side Tilt (Balance) Test

5.1Theory

A body balances on a pivot when its CG is directly above the pivot. In this test the car is tipped sideways onto the tires of one side, so it rests only on the outer edges of those two tires. The car is slowly rotated until it balances and can be held in place with a couple of fingers, with the crane strap slack. At that point the CG lies on the vertical line through the pivot.

In the car's own frame, the CG sits a horizontal distance s from the pivot edge and a height h above the ground plane. When the car is rotated by the balance angle θ, the line from the pivot to the CG is vertical, so the angle between the car's floor and the vertical satisfies tan θ = s / h.

5.2Equations

Because the front and rear of the car have different widths, the width is taken at the lengthwise position of the CG. The fraction of weight on the rear axle sets that position [3]:

d = df + (dr − df) × WR / W

t = tf + (tr − tf) × WR / W

The horizontal distance from the pivot edge to the CG, measured in the car's frame, uses the side-to-side weight split so the result does not assume the car is symmetric. Wup is the weight on the side that gets raised (the right side when tilting onto the left tires):

s = (d − t) / 2 + t × Wup / W

For a symmetric car Wup / W = 0.5 and this simplifies to s = d / 2.

CG height above the ground at static ride height:

h = s / tan θ = s × tan(90° − θ)

where θ is the balance angle, the change in the transverse level reading from level to balanced.

Measurement sensitivity: an error in θ changes h by roughly

Δh ≈ s × Δθ / sin2θ  (Δθ in radians)

so a 0.2° error in the balance angle gave roughly a 0.1 in error in h for the car measured in [3]. Measure the balance angle carefully and repeat it.

5.3Procedure

  1. Complete the pre-test procedure and baseline measurements (Sections 3 and 4).
  2. Place the digital level on the transverse reference spot and record the level reading, θ0.
  3. Chock the lower-side tires so they cannot slide sideways. Place padding under the raised side in case the car drops.
  4. Attach the crane to the raised side and lift slowly. Spotters hold the car on both sides at all times.
  5. Keep raising until the car balances on the outer edges of the lower tires. It should stay in place when held with a couple of fingers, with the crane strap slack.
  6. Confirm the balance point with the plumb bob, then record the level reading at the same reference spot, θbal. The balance angle is θ = |θbal − θ0|.
  7. Lower the car back to level. Repeat steps 4 to 6 for three trials.
  8. If time and safety allow, repeat three trials tilting onto the other side. Averaging both sides cancels out errors in the side-to-side weight split.

6Method B: Rear Lift (Axle Load) Test

6.1Theory

With the front tires on the scales, the rear of the car is lifted by the crane so the car pivots about the front axle. As the car rotates nose-down relative to the rear, the CG moves forward relative to the front axle. The front scales therefore read more weight the higher the CG sits above the axle line. Measuring how much the front axle load increases for a known change in angle gives the CG height [1], [2].

6.2Equations

Front axle weight at each measurement n:

WFn = WFLn + WFRn

CG height above the axle centerline [1]:

ha = L × (WFn − WF1) / ( W × tan(αn − α1) )

where WF1 and α1 are the front axle weight and level reading with the car level, and WFn and αn are the values at the nth lifted position.

CG height above the ground at static ride height, adding the average loaded radius:

h = ha + (rf + rr) / 2

The change in front weight is small compared with the total weight, so scale error can become a large part of the result [3]. Use large angles (20° to 40° of change, raising the front scales on cinder blocks if needed) and take several measurements to average [4].

Note that the tires are not rigid and deform under load, lowering the CG slightly. Even the corrected value is not accurate at speed, mainly because of aero loads and changes in tire pressure [4]. This is part of the reason the tires are overinflated for the test.

6.3Procedure

  1. Complete the pre-test procedure and baseline measurements (Sections 3 and 4).
  2. With the car level, record the front corner weights WFL1 and WFR1, and the level reading at the longitudinal reference spot, α1.
  3. Chock the front wheels so they cannot roll off the scales.
  4. Lift the rear of the car with the crane, keeping the chain as vertical as possible.
  5. Hold the car still and record the level reading αn and the front corner weights WFLn and WFRn.
  6. Repeat steps 4 and 5 at several angles, aiming for about 10°, 20°, 30° and 40° of change.
  7. Lower the car, re-zero the scales, and repeat the full set for a second run.
