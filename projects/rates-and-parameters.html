0Introduction

The goal is to define the suspension rates for ARG27. This page will find:

  • Ride frequency, front and rear
  • Motion ratio, front and rear
  • Wheel rate and spring rate, front and rear
  • Roll gradient and total roll stiffness
  • Roll stiffness distribution between the front and rear
  • Anti-roll bar rates, front and rear
  • Pitch gradient
  • Damping ratio and damper settings, front and rear

1Background

1.1Definitions

TermDefinition
Sprung massEverything held up by the springs: chassis, driver, accumulator, powertrain.
Unsprung massEverything below the springs: wheels, tires, uprights, part of the links.
Motion ratio, MRWheel travel ÷ shock travel. Set by the rocker.
Spring rate, kStiffness of the coil spring at the shock.
Wheel rate, KSpring stiffness felt at the wheel. K = k ÷ MR2.
Tire rateVertical stiffness of the tire. Acts in series with the wheel rate.
Ride frequency, fNatural frequency of one corner bouncing. f = (1 ÷ 2π) √(K ÷ m).
Roll center heightHeight of the point the chassis rolls about. Set by the kinematics.
Roll stiffnessMoment needed to roll the chassis one degree. From springs, anti-roll bars and tires.
Roll gradientDegrees of roll per g of lateral acceleration.
LLTDShare of the lateral load transfer carried by the front axle. Main tool for tuning balance.
ARB rateRoll stiffness added by the anti-roll bar.
ARB motion ratioChassis roll angle per degree of bar twist.
Pitch gradientDegrees of pitch per g of braking or acceleration.
Damping ratio, ζDamping as a fraction of critical damping.
Static sagHow far the damper is compressed at rest, as a fraction of its stroke.
Spring preloadHow far the spring is already compressed with the damper fully extended.

2Spring and Motion Ratio

2.1Process

  1. Get the vehicle design constants.
  2. Get the driving cases and the aero load in each case.
  3. Solve the steady-state load transfer to estimate the load on each tire.
  4. Guess a front and rear spring, motion ratio and preload.
  5. Check damper compression (bottoming out or drooping out) and ride height change.
  6. Iterate on the spring and motion ratio until the checks below pass.

2.2Evaluating a Choice

  • Ride frequency around 3 to 3.3 Hz, the range used on ARG25 and ARG26.
  • Ride frequency difference between front and rear.
  • Aero clearance. With front wing suspension, the front wing mounting point must not hit the ground, and the rear of the car must not bottom out in an acceleration run.

2.4Aero Sensitivity

  • Pitch: the car pitches between −1° and 1°, which changes downforce very little (to be confirmed with CFD).
  • Roll: with front wing suspension the front wing is assumed to have no roll sensitivity, and the rear wing changes little. The undertray is only sensitive when roll brings it close to the ground, where it can shed vortices.

Estimated change in CLA with roll:

ΔCLA = −0.026 per degree

ΔF = 0.5 × ρ × v2 × ΔCLA × roll angle

At 20 m/s and 2° of roll:

ΔF = 0.5 × 1.225 × 202 × (−0.026) × 2 = −12.7 N

That is about 1% of the 957 N of downforce at 20 m/s, so roll sensitivity is small.

BAppendix: Öhlins TTX25 MKII Dampers

ARG26 runs Öhlins TTX25 MKII FSAE dampers. Main details:

  • Twin-tube damper, four-way externally adjustable
  • Stroke of 57 mm (2.24 in)
  • Length of 200 mm between bearing centers, fully extended
  • Weight of 394 g without the spring
  • Spherical bearings with 8 mm ID, 8 mm ball width and 15 mm OD
  • Takes Hyperco springs, sold through dealers such as Essex Racing and KAZ Technologies
  • Rebuild kit for a full car set and revalve kits with different high speed valve springs
  • Dyno plots, CAD models, a workshop manual, spring dimensions and service tools on the Öhlins page
